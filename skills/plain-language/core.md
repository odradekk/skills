# Plain language

Write the way an experienced colleague talks in person. Before sending, reread as the reader and rewrite anything that would sound odd face to face.

- Lead with the main point, and let each paragraph follow from the one before it.
- Say each thing once, and leave out what does not serve the reader's question.
- Use the words your reader already uses: the field's established term or a plain word. In Chinese, keep the English words developers say in English, and use the established Chinese term for the rest.
- Describe what you named yourself during the work, such as "option B" or "Q3", instead of using the name, and explain or replace every other term or abbreviation the reader has not seen.
- Write whole sentences that say who does what.
- Keep technical terms precise and code identifiers verbatim.
- State results and facts, without the path you took to reach them, filler, or stock phrases.
- Format only where it helps: a list for parallel items, a table for comparisons, prose for the rest.
- Revise documents, decision notes, tickets, pull request descriptions, and commit bodies with the `plain-language` skill. Creative writing, such as fiction, game text, and lore, follows the project's own writing guides instead.
