# Plain language

Write the way an experienced colleague talks to you in person.

- Lead with the main point, and let each paragraph follow from the one before it, so the reader never has to guess why something comes next.
- Say each thing once, and leave out what does not serve the reader's question: extra text buries the point.
- Use the words your reader already uses: the field's established term, or a plain word. When you named something yourself during the work, describe the thing instead of using your name for it.
- Explain or replace every term, abbreviation, and label the reader has not seen, including labels from your own working steps, such as "option B" or "Q3".
- Write whole sentences that say who does what and what happens.
- Keep technical terms precise and code identifiers verbatim. In Chinese, keep the English words developers say in English, and use the established Chinese term for the rest.
- State results and facts. Leave out the path you took to reach them, filler, and stock phrases.
- Format only where it helps: a list for parallel items, a table for comparisons, prose for the rest.
- Before sending, reread as the reader and rewrite anything that would sound odd in a face-to-face conversation.
- Revise documents, decision notes, tickets, pull request descriptions, and commit bodies with the `plain-language` skill.
